How it works

01
Scan a receipt. The fields are read for you — vendor, date, tax, total. Anything the model was unsure about is marked, and you correct it before saving.
02
Seal the month. Your expenses are put into one fixed canonical form, hashed with SHA-256, and that fingerprint is written to Solana in a memo you sign with your own wallet.
03
Send the report. Export a CSV. Your reviewer drops it into the form below, which rebuilds the fingerprint in their browser and compares it to the chain.

What sealing proves

That the report in front of your reviewer is byte-for-byte the one you sealed, at the moment that transaction landed.

It does not prove the receipts are genuine, that nothing was left out before sealing, or that the amounts are fair. It is a tamper check on a document, not an audit.

The reviewer never has to trust the claimant's word about the fingerprint either — the page below ignores the fingerprint written in the file and recomputes it from the expense rows.

Questions

Is this real money?
No. SnapClaim runs on Solana devnet. The tokens have no value and the app says so on every screen.
Where do my receipt images go?
Photographs are sent to an AI provider to be read, and the app tells you so before you connect a wallet. Expenses are stored on your phone against your wallet address. You can delete any expense and its image.
Does SnapClaim hold my keys?
No. Signing happens inside your own wallet app through the Mobile Wallet Adapter. SnapClaim never sees a private key, and sealing moves no funds beyond the network fee.
What if the receipt is read wrong?
Every field is editable before you save, and uncertain ones are flagged. If parsing fails entirely you can type the fields yourself — the receipt is never lost.
Which phones does it run on?
Android, with a wallet app that supports the Mobile Wallet Adapter. It is not on the Solana dApp Store yet.
